# Map Snapshot Memo: 1 CE

Destination: `/docs/slates/snapshot-0001-memo.md`, with `/docs/slates/ce-0001.geojson`
as its output. Authored by the owner's reviewer. Second live era.

## Method and rulings

1. Realms are composed from whole Natural Earth 110m units; at this era's
   resolution no cut lines are needed. Fifteen features; every border is a
   scholarly convention and the cartouche's standing caveat covers it.
2. Each realm cites its tradition's default T1 in its sources array, as encoded
   in the geojson.
3. **The headline is the empty space.** Most of the 1 CE world practiced
   traditions outside this museum's launch ten: Greco-Roman, Egyptian, Celtic,
   Germanic, steppe, African, American, and Oceanian religion all render in the
   land tone. This is the outside-scope treatment doing its principal work, and
   the era's methodology note (below) must say so.
4. Anachronism honesty, encoded as grades and notes rather than invented
   categories: the Indian realm colors as Hinduism at grade C, meaning the
   Vedic-Brahmanic religion of the era alongside strong Buddhist and Jain
   presence, with no claim of a settled majority. Gandhara and Bactria
   (Pakistan, Afghanistan) color Buddhist at grade C for the same reason. Japan
   colors as Shinto at grade C: kami worship is attested archaeologically, and
   "Shinto" is a retrospective name the deep dive explains.
5. Absences that must stay absent: Christianity does not exist at 1 CE and has
   no realm; Islam and Sikhism are centuries and millennia away. Jainism is
   present within the Indian realm's mix but holds no majority realm. The era
   note names these plainly.

## Era note for the cartouche or methodology page (owner copy, verbatim)

"At 1 CE the museum's ten traditions cover a minority of the inhabited world.
The land tone is not emptiness: it is Greco-Roman, Egyptian, Celtic, Germanic,
steppe, African, American, and Oceanian religious life, outside this museum's
launch scope. Christianity does not yet exist; Islam and Sikhism are centuries
away. Where a color does appear, its grade carries the uncertainty: the Indian
realm is the Brahmanic religion of its era amid Buddhist and Jain strength, and
Japan's color names practices only later called Shinto."
