# Glossary Slate

Destination: `/docs/slates/glossary-terms.md`. Authored by the owner's reviewer.
Each term cites its tradition's default T1 under the standing rule; cross-tradition
terms cite each carried tradition's T1. Definitions land verbatim as tap-card text.

| term | traditions | definition |
|---|---|---|
| Torah | judaism | The five books of Moses, and by extension the whole of Jewish teaching, written and oral. |
| Talmud | judaism | The Mishnah and its commentary, the Gemara; rabbinic Judaism's central work of law and debate. |
| Halakha | judaism | The path of Jewish law derived from Torah and rabbinic interpretation. |
| Midrash | judaism | Rabbinic interpretation that reads scripture closely and imaginatively for law and meaning. |
| Kabbalah | judaism | The Jewish mystical tradition centered on the Zohar and its map of the divine emanations. |
| Shabbat | judaism | The seventh-day rest, from Friday sunset to Saturday nightfall, at the heart of Jewish practice. |
| Kosher | judaism | Fit under the dietary laws; the system of permitted foods and their preparation. |
| Mitzvah | judaism | A commandment; colloquially, a good deed done as one. |
| Eucharist | christianity | The rite of bread and wine instituted at the Last Supper; its meaning is a defining dispute among branches. |
| Trinity | christianity | The doctrine of one God in three persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. |
| Sacrament | christianity | A rite held to convey grace; counted as seven in Catholic and Orthodox Christianity, fewer in most Protestant branches. |
| Liturgy | christianity | The ordered public worship of the church, from the Divine Liturgy of the East to the mass of the West. |
| Icon | christianity | A sacred image, venerated in Orthodox practice as a window to what it depicts. |
| Grace | christianity | God's unmerited favor; how it saves is the Reformation's central question. |
| Monasticism | christianity, buddhism | The renunciant life under rule; a structural pillar of both Christian and Buddhist history. |
| Quran | islam | The revelation recited to Muhammad, held to be God's literal speech in Arabic. |
| Hadith | islam | A transmitted report of the Prophet's words or deeds; the raw material of the Sunna. |
| Sunna | islam | The Prophet's normative example, followed alongside the Quran. |
| Sharia | islam | The divinely guided path, elaborated as law by the schools of jurisprudence. |
| Sufism | islam | Islam's mystical current of purification, remembrance, and nearness to God. |
| Hajj | islam | The pilgrimage to Mecca, obligatory once for those able, one of the five pillars. |
| Salat | islam | The five daily ritual prayers facing Mecca. |
| Ramadan | islam | The month of dawn-to-dusk fasting commemorating the Quran's first revelation. |
| Ummah | islam | The worldwide community of Muslims. |
| Imam | islam | A prayer leader; in Shia Islam, the divinely guided successor of the Prophet. |
| Dharma | hinduism, buddhism, jainism, sikhism | Cosmic order and right duty in Hindu usage; the Buddha's teaching in Buddhist usage; each tradition inflects the word its own way. |
| Karma | hinduism, buddhism, jainism, sikhism | Action and its fruits binding beings across lives; in Jain teaching, a subtle substance that adheres to the soul. |
| Moksha | hinduism, jainism | Liberation from the cycle of rebirth, the final aim. |
| Samsara | hinduism, buddhism, jainism, sikhism | The wandering cycle of birth, death, and rebirth. |
| Brahman | hinduism | The ultimate reality of the Upanishads, of which all things are expressions. |
| Atman | hinduism | The self or soul; its relation to Brahman divides the Vedanta schools. |
| Bhakti | hinduism, sikhism | Loving devotion to the divine, the current that remade Indian religion. |
| Puja | hinduism, jainism | Ritual worship of a deity or venerated being with offerings, at home or in the temple. |
| Nirvana | buddhism | The extinguishing of craving and release from the cycle; the Buddhist goal. |
| Sangha | buddhism | The community, in the narrow sense the ordained monastics who preserve the teaching. |
| Bodhisattva | buddhism | In Mahayana, a being vowed to awaken for the sake of all others. |
| Sutra | buddhism, hinduism | A discourse of the Buddha in Buddhist usage; an aphoristic treatise in Hindu usage. |
| Zen | buddhism | The meditation school (Chan in China) holding awakening to be pointed at directly, beyond words. |
| Khalsa | sikhism | The initiated Sikh order founded in 1699, bearing the five articles of faith. |
| Langar | sikhism | The free community kitchen of the gurdwara, where all eat together as equals. |
| Gurdwara | sikhism | The Sikh place of worship, the door of the Guru, housing the Guru Granth Sahib. |
| Kirtan | sikhism | Congregational singing of the scripture's hymns. |
| Dao | chinese-traditions | The Way: the unnameable source and pattern of things in Taoist teaching, the moral way in Confucian usage. |
| Wu-wei | chinese-traditions | Effortless action that accords with the Way rather than forcing against it. |
| Yin-yang | chinese-traditions | The complementary polarities whose interplay patterns the cosmos. |
| Ren | chinese-traditions | Confucian humaneness, the perfected disposition toward others. |
| Li | chinese-traditions | Ritual propriety; the forms through which Confucian virtue is practiced. |
| Qi | chinese-traditions | The vital energy or breath pervading body and cosmos. |
| Kami | shinto | The venerated presences of Shinto, from great deities to the spirits of places and ancestors. |
| Matsuri | shinto | A shrine festival, the communal celebration at the heart of Shinto practice. |
| Torii | shinto | The gate marking passage into a shrine's sacred space. |
| Ahimsa | jainism, hinduism, buddhism | Nonviolence toward all beings; supreme in Jain ethics and shared across the Indian traditions. |
| Anekantavada | jainism | The Jain doctrine of many-sidedness: truth has more aspects than any single view. |
| Tirthankara | jainism | A ford-maker: one of the twenty-four teachers who show the crossing to liberation. |
| Avesta | zoroastrianism | The Zoroastrian scriptures, with the Gathas at their oldest core. |
| Gathas | zoroastrianism | The hymns attributed to Zarathustra himself, the tradition's founding voice. |
| Magi | zoroastrianism | The hereditary priesthood of the Iranian tradition. |
