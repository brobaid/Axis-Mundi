# Map Snapshot Memo: 600 CE

Destination: `/docs/slates/snapshot-0600-memo.md`, with `/docs/slates/ce-0600.geojson`
as its output. Authored by the owner's reviewer. Third live era.

## Method and rulings

1. Forty-nine realms from whole Natural Earth units, same conventions as 1 CE.
   Realm sources cite the era-appropriate tradition T1s as encoded (Christianity
   at this era cites oxford-early-christian-studies-2008).
2. **The headline absence: Islam.** At 600 CE the first revelation is a decade
   away and the Hijra two decades; Arabia renders in land tone, its polytheism
   outside scope. Within thirty years of this frame, most of its eastern half
   changes color. The era note must say this; it is the map's single most
   arresting fact.
3. The Christian world at its pre-Islamic widest: Byzantine Anatolia, Syria,
   Egypt, and North Africa all render Christian, grades B and C toward the
   Berber west; Nubia and Aksum carry the faith up the Nile and into the horn.
   The Balkans north of Greece render outside scope: the Slavic migrations have
   made them pagan at this frame, and honesty beats tidiness.
4. England is grade C: Augustine's mission is three years old at Canterbury
   while the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms remain largely pagan and Celtic Christianity
   holds the west; the hatching is doing exactly its job.
5. The Sasanian realm stands at full extent a generation before its fall:
   Iran grade A, Mesopotamia B for its great Christian and Jewish populations,
   Sogdiana C for the Silk Road's mixture.
6. Japan is grade B Shinto: the Buddhist transmission is fifty years old at
   court while kami practice remains the common religion; Korea's three
   kingdoms render Buddhist at B. China renders as the cluster at A under the
   standing convention, with Sui Buddhism noted in the era note.
7. No Judaism realm at this era: Jewish life is wholly diasporic between the
   revolts and the exilarchate, and the map must not invent a majority where
   the sources place none. The era note carries this.

## Era note (owner copy, verbatim)

"600 CE is the world on the eve. Islam does not yet exist: the first revelation
comes in 610, the Hijra in 622, and within a century the map from Spain to the
Indus is redrawn. Here the Christian world stands at its pre-Islamic widest,
from Ireland to Aksum; the Sasanian realm burns its fires a generation before
the fall; north of Greece the Slavic migrations have returned the Balkans to
gods outside this museum's scope; and Jewish life, wholly diasporic, holds no
