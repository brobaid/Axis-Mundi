# Demographics Dataset: All Ten Dives

Destination: `/docs/slates/demographics.md`. Authored by the owner's reviewer.
Fills the demographics section of every dive. Default source: `pew-grl-2025`;
rows marked otherwise add the named source. Bodies land verbatim. The counting
notes for Shinto and the Chinese cluster are part of the content, not hedges to
trim.

## christianity

2.3 billion adherents, 28.8 percent of humanity, the largest tradition on
earth and, over 2010-2020, the one whose share fell fastest, down 1.8 points.
Its center of gravity has moved south: sub-Saharan Africa is now home to
almost a third of all Christians, more than any other region, while Europe
and North America decline through disaffiliation. The largest Christian
populations are in the United States, Brazil, Mexico, Russia, and the
Philippines; the fastest growth is African.

## islam

2.0 billion adherents, 25.6 percent of humanity, the second-largest tradition
and the fastest-growing over 2010-2020, up 1.8 points of share, driven by the
youngest age profile of any major religion. Its demographic heart is not the
Arab world: the largest Muslim populations are in Indonesia, Pakistan, India,
and Bangladesh, and South and Southeast Asia together far outweigh the Middle
East. Sub-Saharan Africa is its fastest-growing region.

## hinduism

1.2 billion adherents, 14.9 percent of humanity, growing in step with world
population and so holding share steady. It is the most geographically
concentrated of the large traditions: about 95 percent of Hindus live in
India, with Nepal the only other Hindu-majority state, and the modern
diaspora, from the Gulf to North America, Britain, and the Caribbean, though
culturally prominent, is a small fraction of the whole.

## buddhism

324 million adherents by affiliation, 4.1 percent of humanity, and the only
major tradition whose absolute numbers fell over 2010-2020, through low
birthrates and disaffiliation in East Asia. Affiliation understates it:
across China, Japan, and Korea, Buddhist practice lives inside plural
religious lives that censuses cannot count. Its majority states run through
Southeast Asia, Thailand, Myanmar, Cambodia, Sri Lanka, Laos, and Bhutan,
with Mongolia the northern outpost.

## sikhism

Roughly 26 million adherents (india-census-2011 with projection; add this
source). The great majority live in India, concentrated in Punjab, the one
Sikh-majority state in the world at the sub-national level. The diaspora,
above all in Canada, Britain, and the United States, is proportionally the
largest of any Indian-origin tradition and increasingly shapes the
community's public life.

## chinese

No single figure is honest here, and this museum keeps the cluster's count as
a note by design (taxonomy rule). Pew's category assignments render most of
mainland China religiously unaffiliated while folk practice, ancestor rites,
and temple religion involve hundreds of millions; Taiwan is the one polity
where the traditions render as a plurality. Estimates for folk-religion
participants alone run in the hundreds of millions, and Confucian observance
is not countable as membership at all.

## shinto

Contested by definition, and counted here with a note rather than a number
(japan-aca-yearbook; add this source). Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs
tallies some 80 to 90 million shrine parishioners by shrine reporting, while
surveys find most Japanese claiming no religion; both are true, because
Shinto is practice woven into place and season rather than membership. The
2020 map renders Japan unaffiliated for exactly this reason, and the
methodology page carries the explanation.

## jainism

About 4.5 million by India's census, which the community itself considers an
undercount (india-census-2011; add this source), concentrated in Gujarat,
Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and Karnataka, with a prosperous diaspora in North
America, Britain, and East Africa. Among the smallest of the world's old
traditions and among the most literate and prosperous communities in India by
every census measure.

## zoroastrianism

Roughly 110 to 120 thousand worldwide (fezana-demographics; add this source),
the smallest tradition in this museum: the Parsis of India, some 50 to 60
thousand and declining by about a tenth each decade through late marriage,
low birthrates, and patrilineal membership rules; Iran's community in the
tens of thousands; and a diaspora, led by North America, that now rivals the
homelands. Whether to accept converts is the community's most consequential
open question, and this museum's matrix carries it as a contested cell.

## judaism

14.8 million adherents, 0.2 percent of humanity, with a distribution shaped
by the twentieth century: nearly half in Israel, the world's one
Jewish-majority state since 1948, most of the remainder in the United States,
and the old European heartlands reduced to small communities. The population
has still not returned to its pre-Shoah peak of some 16.5 million; Israel's
Jewish population grows while most diaspora communities hold steady or
decline.
