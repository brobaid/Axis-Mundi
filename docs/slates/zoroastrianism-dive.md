## overview (sourcing: sourced)

Zoroastrianism is the religion of the [[gathas|Gathas]]: the hymns of
Zarathustra, who proclaimed Ahura Mazda, the Wise Lord, wholly good, and the
world as the arena of a real contest between truth and the lie in which every
human choice matters. Once the faith of three Iranian empires, it survives
today in perhaps 110 to 120 thousand adherents, the Parsis of India, the
Zoroastrians of Iran, and a growing diaspora, making it the smallest tradition
in this museum and among the oldest. Its ethic is the tradition's own
summary: good thoughts, good words, good deeds; its emblem is the fire kept
burning in its temples, symbol of the divine light, and many scholars have
argued for its influence on the eschatology of the Abrahamic faiths, in
degrees still debated.

## origins (sourcing: sourced)

The Gathas are the beginning, and their date is the tradition's oldest open
question: linguistic dating places Zarathustra around 1500-1000 BCE on the
Central Asian steppe, while a later tradition set him in the sixth century
BCE, and this museum carries both positions. The Achaemenid kings invoked
Ahura Mazda over the first world empire; the Sasanians built a church of the
realm, fixed the [[avesta|Avesta]] in writing, and made the fires imperial.
The Arab conquest of the seventh century began a long demotion from majority
to minority: by the millennium Iran was Muslim, and the ninth-century Pahlavi
books preserved the theology through the change. One migration, remembered in
the Qissa-ye Sanjan, carried the faith to Gujarat, where the Parsis became
colonial India's most prominent small community; Iran's Zoroastrians endured
at Yazd and Kerman, emancipated in 1882, and the twentieth century scattered
both branches into a worldwide diaspora.

## core_beliefs (sourcing: sourced)

Ahura Mazda is wholly good, creator of a good world; evil is real, the work of
the hostile spirit, and not God's, which is the tradition's answer to the
problem other monotheisms carry. History is the contest's field and it ends in
renovation: evil defeated, the dead raised, the world made fresh, among the
oldest articulated eschatologies on record. The human being is a combatant by
birthright: free, responsible, and enlisted through good thoughts, good
words, and good deeds; at death the soul crosses the account-keeper's bridge,
wide for the just and knife-edged for the wicked. Fire is the symbol and
direction of worship, never its object; purity guards creation's good order;
and despair is, in the tradition's own tenor, a kind of desertion.

## practices (five entries)

1. kusti-prayers: The sacred cord untied and retied with prayers at the
   day's five watches, the initiated Zoroastrian's constant discipline.
2. fire-reverence: Prayer before flame, from hearth to the great Atash
   Behrams whose fires have burned for centuries.
3. gahambars: The six seasonal feasts of obligation, thanksgiving, and
   communal meal.
4. purity: The care of fire, water, and earth from pollution, the ritual
   architecture of the tradition.
5. charity: Ashodad, righteous giving; the community's philanthropies have
   long outrun its numbers.

## rites (four entries)

1. birth: The newborn is welcomed with prayers and the household fire kept
   bright; formal entry waits for initiation.
2. childhood: Navjote (sedreh-pushi in Iran): between about seven and twelve
   the child is invested with the sacred shirt and cord, taking on the
   religion's obligations.
3. marriage: The ashirvad blessings before witnesses, sweetened with rice and
   rose petals.
4. death: Rites keep the elements unpolluted: the towers of silence where
   tradition and law allow, burial or cremation increasingly elsewhere, a
   change the community itself debates; the soul's crossing is prayed for on
   the fourth dawn.

## misconceptions

| id | claim | correction |
|---|---|---|
| fire-worship | Zoroastrians worship fire. | Fire is the symbol and direction of prayer, as light toward the Wise Lord; worship belongs to Ahura Mazda alone. |
| two-equal-gods | Zoroastrian dualism means two equal gods. | Ahura Mazda is supreme and evil's spirit is neither his equal nor eternal; the contest is real but its end is not in doubt. |
| extinct-religion | Zoroastrianism is extinct. | It is small and alive: fires tended, navjotes performed, and a diaspora now rivaling the homelands, with its demographic future the community's own most debated question. |
