## overview (replaces stub; sourcing: sourced)

Islam holds that God is one, without partner, likeness, or need, and that the
[[quran|Quran]] is God's final revelation, delivered through Muhammad, the seal
of a line of prophets that includes Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. The
word islam means submission: the fitting response of the creature to the
Creator, and the religion's name for itself describes an act rather than a
founder. A Muslim's life is structured by five obligatory practices, from the
profession of faith to the pilgrimage at Mecca, and by a moral law understood
to cover all of life. From one city in seventh-century Arabia, Islam grew
within a century into a civilization reaching from the Atlantic to the Indus;
today its two billion adherents make it the world's second-largest religion and
its fastest-growing, with its demographic heart in South and Southeast Asia
rather than the Arab lands of its birth.

## origins (replaces stub; sourcing: sourced)

According to Islamic tradition, revelation began in 610 CE when Muhammad, a
forty-year-old merchant of Mecca in retreat on Mount Hira, was commanded by the
angel Gabriel to recite. The revelations continued for twenty-three years, and
their gathered text is the Quran. Mecca's ruling clans met the message of one
God and a coming judgment with escalating hostility, and in 622 the small
community migrated north to Yathrib, thereafter Medina: the [[hijra|Hijra]],
from which the Islamic calendar counts. In Medina the community became a
polity, and the decade that followed brought the battles, treaties, and
conversions through which Mecca itself entered Islam without a sack in 630. By
Muhammad's death in 632 most of Arabia had joined the new order, and the
question his death left open, who rightly leads the community, became in time
the deepest division within it: those who held that leadership passed to the
community's chosen caliphs became the Sunni majority, and those who held that
it belonged to the Prophet's house through Ali became the Shia.

## core_beliefs (replaces stub; sourcing: sourced)

At the center is [[tawhid]], the absolute oneness of God: the sin Islam calls
unforgivable if unrepented is shirk, associating partners with God, and the
tradition's art, law, and mysticism are all shaped by that refusal. Faith is
classically summarized in six articles: God; the angels; the revealed books,
of which the Quran is the final and protected form; the prophets, culminating
in Muhammad; the Day of Judgment, when every soul is weighed; and the divine
decree, God's encompassing knowledge and will. The human being is not fallen
but forgetful, and revelation is a reminder; each person is born capable of
submission and answerable for their own deeds, with no intermediary between
the worshipper and God. Paradise and fire are described with concrete vividness
according to the Quran, and God's mercy is repeatedly declared to outrun his
wrath.

## practices (five entries, in this order)

1. shahada: The profession of faith: there is no god but God, and Muhammad is
   the messenger of God. Saying it with intent is how one enters Islam.
2. salat: The five daily prayers facing Mecca, from dawn to night, in
   prescribed words and movements; Friday's midday prayer is congregational.
3. zakat: The obligatory alms, classically a fortieth of qualifying wealth
   each year, owed to the poor and other named categories.
4. sawm: The dawn-to-dusk fast of [[ramadan]], abstaining from food, drink,
   and marital relations, commemorating the Quran's first descent.
5. hajj: The pilgrimage to Mecca in the sacred month, obligatory once for
   every Muslim able to perform it, ending in the feast of sacrifice.

## rites (four entries)

1. birth: The call to prayer is whispered into the newborn's ear; on the
   seventh day many families name the child and offer the aqiqah sacrifice.
2. circumcision: Male circumcision is near-universal practice, its timing
   varying by region and school.
3. marriage: The nikah is a contract before witnesses with an agreed gift to
   the bride; it is a solemn covenant but not a sacrament.
4. funeral: The dead are washed, shrouded, prayed over in the janazah prayer,
   and buried facing Mecca, ideally before the day ends; mourning is
   restrained and cremation is prohibited.

| jihad-means-holy-war | "Jihad" simply means holy war. | The word means striving. It covers the moral struggle of the self and, in law, regulated armed conflict with strict limits; equating it with indiscriminate war is rejected by mainstream scholarship in every school. |
