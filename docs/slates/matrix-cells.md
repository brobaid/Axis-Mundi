# Matrix Cells Dataset: The Nine Remaining Dimensions

Destination: `/docs/slates/matrix-cells.md`. Authored by the owner's reviewer.
Ninety cells: nine dimensions by ten traditions. Values are enum-ish for
filter chips; nuance is the cell popover text and lands verbatim.

## Rulings

1. Sources: each cell cites its tradition's default T1; Christianity cells cite
   `macculloch-christianity-2009`; Chinese cells cite both Chinese T1s. All add
   `britannica` only where a cell names a specific practice with its own entry;
   otherwise the T1 alone satisfies spec 9.2.2.
2. Two contested cells, marked below, carry both positions verbatim.
3. "Varies" is a legitimate value and earns a filter chip like any other under
   the earned-chip rule.

## Cosmology and creation

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Created by God | false | Genesis frames creation by divine word; rabbinic tradition holds literal and non-literal readings side by side. |
| christianity | Created by God | false | Creation from nothing by God; branches divide over literal readings of Genesis. |
| islam | Created by God | false | The world exists by God's command; the Quran's six days are widely read as ages. |
| hinduism | Cyclical | false | Endless cycles of manifestation and dissolution across vast kalpas; multiple creation accounts are held together without contradiction. |
| buddhism | Cyclical, no creator | false | World systems arise and pass beginninglessly; the Buddha set creation questions aside as not conducive to liberation. |
| sikhism | Created by God | false | Creation unfolds from the One's command (hukam); scripture speaks of measureless ages before it. |
| chinese-traditions | Varies | false | Taoist cosmogony unfolds spontaneously from primordial unity; Confucian thought is largely silent; folk cosmologies are many. |
| shinto | Generative myth | false | The kami generate the islands and the world in the chronicles; there is no doctrine of creation from nothing. |
| jainism | Eternal, no creator | false | The universe is uncreated and everlasting, moving through cosmic half-cycles. |
| zoroastrianism | Created, contested by evil | false | Ahura Mazda creates a wholly good world that the hostile spirit assaults; history is the arena of their conflict. |

## Path to salvation or liberation

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Covenant faithfulness | false | Life according to Torah and the commandments; the tradition's weight falls on sanctifying this life more than on salvation from it. |
| christianity | Grace through Christ | false | Salvation through Christ; the branches' defining disputes concern faith, works, and sacraments. |
| islam | Faith and works | false | Affirming God's oneness, keeping the pillars, and righteous deeds, weighed at the judgment with God's mercy decisive. |
| hinduism | Multiple paths | false | Knowledge, devotion, action, and discipline are recognized margas to moksha; schools rank them differently. |
| buddhism | The eightfold path | false | Ethics, meditation, and wisdom uproot craving; nirvana is release from the cycle. |
| sikhism | Remembrance and grace | false | Remembrance of the Name, honest work, and service dissolve self-centeredness; the Guru's grace completes it. |
| chinese-traditions | Varies | false | Confucian cultivation aims at sagehood in this world; Taoist practice at alignment with the Way or transcendence; folk religion at merit and blessing. |
| shinto | Purity, not salvation | false | There is no salvation doctrine; purification and right relation with the kami order this life. |
| jainism | Ascetic self-purification | false | Liberation comes by halting and shedding karma through nonviolence, truthfulness, and austerity. |
| zoroastrianism | Good thoughts, words, deeds | false | The threefold ethic aligns the soul with truth for the crossing after death and the world's renovation. |

## Scripture and authority

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Torah and oral law | false | Written and oral Torah together; rabbinic interpretation is authoritative in the rabbinic streams, while Karaites hold to scripture alone. |
| christianity | Bible; authority varies | false | Scripture read with tradition and magisterium in Catholic and Orthodox Christianity, or as sole authority in Protestant principle. |
| islam | Quran and Sunna | false | The Quran is supreme, interpreted with the Prophet's practice as carried by the hadith and the law schools. |
| hinduism | Shruti and smriti | false | The Vedas are nominally supreme; in practice the epics, Puranas, and teachers' lineages carry living authority. |
| buddhism | Canon and lineage | false | Canons differ by branch, and teacher-to-student lineage carries weight, most strongly in Mahayana and Vajrayana. |
| sikhism | Guru Granth Sahib | false | The scripture is the living Guru; no human authority stands above it. |
| chinese-traditions | Classics and canons | false | The Confucian classics, the Taoist canon, and folk transmission run in parallel; no single scripture governs the cluster. |
| shinto | No canonical scripture | false | The chronicles and ritual norms guide practice; shrine tradition, not revealed text, carries authority. |
| jainism | Agamas, disputed canon | true | Position 1: Svetambara tradition holds the surviving Agamas to be the authentic canon. Position 2: Digambara tradition holds the original canon lost and relies on later digests. Both revere the Tattvartha Sutra. |
| zoroastrianism | Avesta | false | The Avesta, transmitted by the priesthood, with the Pahlavi books as its theological companion. |

## Clergy and leadership

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Rabbis | false | Teacher-jurists rather than sacramental priests; ordination certifies learning. |
| christianity | Varies by branch | false | From sacramental priesthood under bishops to congregational ministry, the range is the branch map itself. |
| islam | Scholars, no priesthood | false | The ulama interpret; Twelver Shiism adds a graded clerical hierarchy; no sacraments require a priest. |
| hinduism | Priests and gurus | false | Temple priesthood, largely hereditary, runs parallel to renunciant orders and the guru-disciple line. |
| buddhism | Monastic sangha | false | The ordained community is the tradition's core institution; lay teaching roles vary by branch. |
| sikhism | No clergy | false | Granthis read and serve; authority rests in the scripture and the community. |
| chinese-traditions | Varies | false | Ordained Taoist priests, the historical Confucian literati, and folk temple keepers and spirit-mediums each lead their sphere. |
| shinto | Shrine priesthood | false | Kannushi maintain shrines and conduct rites, often hereditarily. |
| jainism | Mendicant orders | false | Monks and nuns under acharyas; the laity is organized around their example and instruction. |
| zoroastrianism | Hereditary priesthood | false | Mobeds from priestly families tend the fires and perform the rites. |

## Worship form and rhythm

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Daily prayer, Shabbat | false | Thrice-daily prayer, Torah reading, and the Shabbat and festival cycle. |
| christianity | Weekly congregational | false | Sunday worship, eucharistic or preaching-centered by branch, over a liturgical year. |
| islam | Five daily prayers | false | Salat five times daily, Friday congregation, and the Ramadan fast. |
| hinduism | Puja, home and temple | false | Household puja, temple darshan, and the festival calendar; no fixed congregational day. |
| buddhism | Offerings and meditation | false | Offerings, chanting, and meditation, with uposatha observances; forms differ widely by branch. |
| sikhism | Sangat and kirtan | false | Congregational hymn-singing and scripture in the gurdwara, daily nitnem, and langar. |
| chinese-traditions | Festival and occasion | false | Temple offerings, ancestor rites, and the festival cycle rather than weekly congregation. |
| shinto | Shrine visits and matsuri | false | Purification and offerings at shrines, life-event visits, and festival processions. |
| jainism | Temple rites and fasting | false | Image worship or aniconic assembly by order, with confession and fasting cycles. |
| zoroastrianism | Fire and hearth prayers | false | Prayers before fire through the day's five watches; temple rites are priest-led. |

|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Varies by observance | false | Head covering, tzitzit, and modesty norms strongest in Orthodox streams. |

| jainism | Ascetic codes | false | Digambara monks renounce clothing, Svetambara wear white; some orders veil the mouth; lay dress follows custom. |
| zoroastrianism | Sudreh and kusti | false | The initiated wear the sacred shirt and cord beneath ordinary dress. |

## Conversion and missionary stance

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Accepts, does not seek | false | Conversion is possible and honored once made; there is no mission to gain converts. |
| christianity | Missionary | false | Evangelism is mandated in most branches and central to the tradition's history. |
| islam | Missionary | false | Dawa, the invitation to Islam, is enjoined; entry is by profession of faith. |
| hinduism | Traditionally non-missionary | false | Historically birth-based; several modern movements accept and some actively seek converts. |
| buddhism | Teaching, not proselytizing | false | The dharma is offered to all and has traveled by mission, without a conversion imperative on individuals. |
| sikhism | Open, non-proselytizing | false | Entrants are accepted; organized mission is not central. |
| chinese-traditions | Non-missionary | false | Participation follows family, custom, and locality rather than conversion. |
| shinto | Non-missionary | false | Bound to place and community; conversion is not an operative concept. |
| jainism | Non-missionary | false | Open in principle, a birth community in practice. |
| zoroastrianism | Contested | true | Position 1: dominant Parsi tradition does not accept converts, tying the religion to descent. Position 2: Iranian practice and much of the diaspora accept conversion, and the dispute is live within the community. |

## Core ethical frame

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Covenant and mitzvot | false | The commandments as lived ethics, with justice and lovingkindness at the center. |
| christianity | The love command | false | Love of God and neighbor as the law's summary, elaborated through virtue and conscience traditions. |
| islam | Guided virtue | false | Divine guidance spanning worship and dealings, with justice and mercy as constant demands. |
| hinduism | Dharma | false | Duty relative to station and stage of life, with ahimsa and hospitality broadly held. |
| buddhism | Precepts and compassion | false | The five precepts and non-harm, with compassion inseparable from wisdom. |
| sikhism | Truthful living | false | Honest labor, sharing, and service, with caste distinction rejected. |
| chinese-traditions | Relational virtue | false | Filiality, reciprocity, and humaneness in the Confucian stream; naturalness and non-contention in the Taoist. |
| shinto | Sincerity and purity | false | Makoto and the avoidance of pollution rather than a codified ethic. |
| jainism | Ahimsa supreme | false | Nonviolence extended to all beings, joined to truthfulness and non-possession. |
| zoroastrianism | Truth against the lie | false | Good thoughts, words, and deeds as active partisanship for truth and the world's betterment. |

## View of other religions

| tradition | value | contested | nuance |
|---|---|---|---|
| judaism | Non-exclusive for others | false | The righteous of all nations have a share; the Noahide frame asks ethics, not conversion, of others. |
| christianity | Varies by branch | false | Positions run from exclusivist to inclusivist; Nostra Aetate reset the Catholic stance in 1965. |
| islam | People of the Book | false | Jews and Christians hold recognized standing as recipients of revelation, with Islam as its completion. |
| hinduism | Many paths | false | Truth is one and the wise call it by many names is a commonly held frame; schools still rank paths internally. |
| buddhism | Judged by fruits | false | Other teachings are weighed pragmatically; the dharma is held complete without requiring others' condemnation. |
| sikhism | Truth beyond labels | false | The One transcends Hindu and Muslim; the scripture itself includes saints of other traditions. |
| chinese-traditions | Complementary teachings | false | The three-teachings frame made coexistence the historical norm. |
| shinto | Coexistent | false | A millennium of amalgamation with Buddhism made exclusivity foreign outside the State Shinto era. |
| jainism | Many-sidedness | false | Anekantavada treats rival views as partial perspectives rather than plain falsehoods. |
| zoroastrianism | Dualist frame, varied practice | false | The sharp truth-lie cosmology historically coexisted with a range of treatment of other communities. |
