## overview (sourcing: sourced)

This museum treats Chinese religion as one ecology with three named streams
and a vast common ground: Confucian self-cultivation and social ethics, Taoist
alignment with the [[dao|Way]], and the folk religion of temples, ancestors,
and local gods in which both are rooted. Membership is not exclusive and never
has been: the same family honors its ancestors, consults a temple god, buries
with Buddhist rites, and orders its obligations by Confucian norms, which is
why this site counts the cluster with a note rather than a single figure. It
is among the oldest continuous religious cultures on earth, documented from
the oracle bones of the second millennium BCE, suppressed in the twentieth
century's middle decades, and visibly reviving since.

## origins (sourcing: sourced)

The bedrock is ancestral: the Shang kings divined and sacrificed to their
dead, and the Zhou framed rule itself as Heaven's mandate held by virtue. The
axial masters gave the ecology its named streams: Confucius taught ritual
propriety and humaneness as the repair of a disordered age; the Daodejing,
attributed to the legendary Laozi, taught the Way that acts by not forcing.
The Han married the Confucian canon to the state, and organized Taoism began
with the Celestial Masters in 142 CE; Buddhism's arrival completed the "three
teachings" that would share the culture for two millennia. Neo-Confucianism
under Zhu Xi renewed the first stream and ruled the examinations into 1905;
the twentieth century abolished, attacked, and finally half-released the whole
inheritance, which now revives on the mainland, and never left Taiwan.

## core_beliefs (sourcing: sourced)

Heaven, tian, orders the world morally in the Confucian stream; the [[dao]]
precedes and patterns it in the Taoist; and in the temples the two share, the
cosmos runs on reciprocity between the living, the ancestors, and the gods,
many of them exemplary humans deified. The person is a knot of relations, and
virtue is relational: filiality, [[ren|humaneness]], and [[li|ritual
propriety]] in the Confucian account; naturalness, simplicity, and [[wu-wei]]
in the Taoist. [[qi]] pervades body and world, and cultivation, whether
Confucian study, Taoist internal alchemy, or a morning's taiji, tunes the
person to the whole. There is no single doctrine of salvation because the
ecology never needed one: harmony, longevity, ancestral continuity, and
sagehood are its goods.

## practices (five entries)

1. ancestor-veneration: Offerings, tablets, and grave-sweeping bind the
   family across death, the ecology's oldest and widest practice.
2. offerings: Incense, food, and spirit money at temple and home altar,
   the grammar of exchange with gods and ancestors.
3. divination: From the Yijing to temple oracle lots, asking the pattern of
   things before acting.
4. cultivation: Confucian study and self-examination, Taoist internal arts,
   taiji and qigong: the person as lifelong work.
5. temple-festivals: The god's birthday procession and temple fair, where
   community, commerce, and devotion are one event.

## rites (four entries)

1. birth: The full-month celebration presents the child, red eggs shared,
   the name entered in the family's line.
2. coming-of-age: The classical capping and hairpin rites marked adulthood;
   long lapsed, they see deliberate modern revival.
3. marriage: The couple serves tea to elders and the ancestors are informed:
   marriage joins families, not only persons.
4. death: The ecology's greatest rite: graded mourning, burial or increasingly
   cremation, and care of the dead continuing through Qingming's grave-sweeping.

## misconceptions

| id | claim | correction |
|---|---|---|
| confucianism-not-religion | Confucianism is just an ethical philosophy, not a religion. | It has temples, sacrifice, a cult of the sage, and Heaven at its summit; the clean philosophy-religion divide is a modern Western frame the tradition itself never drew. |
| chinese-religion-superstition | Folk practice is mere superstition, not real religion. | The temple ecology is an organized, textured religious system with clergy, canon, calendar, and theology of reciprocity; "superstition" was the label of its twentieth-century suppressors. |
| must-choose-one | A person must be Confucian or Taoist or Buddhist. | Chinese religious life is non-exclusive by structure; the three teachings frame one life together, which is why this museum counts the cluster rather than its parts. |
