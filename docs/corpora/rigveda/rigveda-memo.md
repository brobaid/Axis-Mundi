# Reading Room Corpus: The Rigveda

Destination: `/docs/corpora/rigveda/`. Authored by the owner's reviewer.
Completes the Hindu shelf's first delivery; the scope table's "Rigveda first"
ruling is hereby satisfied.

1. Sanskrit: the samhita text with vedic accent marks preserved (new source
   record `rigveda-samhita-digital`). English: Ralph T. H. Griffith's 1896
   translation, public domain (new source record `griffith-1896`, on-page
   note: "Ralph Griffith's 1896 translation, public domain; its register is
   Victorian and its renderings dated, stated plainly; hymns Griffith left
   untranslated on propriety grounds appear in Sanskrit only").
2. Structure: ten mandalas, 1,028 hymns by the tradition's count; the hymn is
   the citable unit and the route unit (/read/rigveda/<mandala>/<hymn>), with
   each hymn's dedicatory deity carried from Griffith's headers where given.
   Pairing is at hymn level, not verse level, by design: Griffith's stanzas
   and the samhita's rc divisions do not map mechanically, and forcing them
   would fabricate a concordance.
3. Honest residuals, counted: 1,012 hymns paired; 15 Sanskrit-only, clustered
   in Mandala 8 where the Valakhilya apocryphal hymns are absent from the
   English digitization, with singletons in Mandalas 3, 7, and 10; 1
   English-only from a numbering shift. Single-sided treatment throughout;
   the contents page states the counts; a future English source may patch
   the Valakhilya block.
4. The shelf entry states the canon reality per the dive: the Rigveda opens
   the Vedic corpus and does not close it; the Upanishads and the remaining
   samhitas are named as coming rows with the standing waiting-on treatment.
