Overview

Islam holds that God is one, without partner or likeness, and that the Quran is God's final revelation, delivered through Muhammad, the last in a line of prophets that includes Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. The believer's life is structured by five obligatory practices, from daily prayer to the pilgrimage at Mecca.

Origins and history

610–632 CE

According to Islamic tradition, revelation began in 610 CE in a cave outside Mecca and continued for twenty-three years. The community's migration to Medina in 622, the Hijra, marks year one of the Islamic calendar. By Muhammad's death in 632, most of the Arabian Peninsula had entered the new polity.

Common misconceptions

Misconception

"Muslims worship Muhammad."

In Islamic teaching, worship is directed to God alone. Muhammad is revered as the final prophet and moral exemplar, and is not held to be divine; venerating him as such would contradict tawhid itself.

Source: Esposito, Islam: The Straight Path (T1)

First Council of Nicaea

Convened byConstantine I
LocationNicaea, Anatolia
TypeEcumenical council

First ecumenical council of the Christian church. It produced the original Nicene Creed and condemned Arianism, setting the pattern for conciliar authority that shaped every later schism and settlement.
